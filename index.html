A noun names something

A noun is a word for a person, place, thing or idea. Here every noun gets a big black triangle. That is the Montessori grammar symbol for a noun: solid and steady, like a pyramid.

Ask yourself: can I put a, an or the in front of it? If yes, it is probably a noun.

Four kinds of naming

Some nouns you can see and touch. Others, like courage, you cannot touch at all, but they are still nouns.

Personteacher, Priya, pilot, baby
Placelibrary, beach, India, park
Thingpencil, kite, apple, chair
Idealove, courage, joy, freedom

Common and proper nouns

A common noun names any one of a kind: city, river, boy. A proper noun names one particular one: London, Ganges, Arjun. Proper nouns always start with a capital letter.

One or many

A singular noun means one. A plural noun means more than one. Most nouns add -s, but some follow other patterns.

PatternRuleExample
Most nounsadd -scat → cats
Ends in s, x, ch, shadd -esbox → boxes
Consonant + yy becomes -iesbaby → babies
Vowel + yadd -stoy → toys
Ends in f or feoften becomes -vesleaf → leaves
Irregularlearn by heartchild → children, mouse → mice
No changesame for one or manysheep → sheep

A noun's two best friends

A verb is a red circle, like a ball that rolls. It tells what someone does or is: jumped, sings, is.

An adjective is a smaller blue triangle that leans on the noun. It describes the noun: quick, red, lazy.

Small words like the, a, over, my and very get no shape in this game.
